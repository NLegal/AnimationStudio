# Color Standards Guide

## Overview

Color consistency is essential for brand recognition and visual cohesion. This guide defines the approved color palettes for all assets in the Animation Studio. Deviations from these standards require art director approval.

## Per-Category Color Guidelines

### Toys
**Primary approach**: Bright primary colors with high saturation.
**Avoid**: Muted tones, dark colors, or monochromatic schemes.
**Best practices**: Use 2-3 contrasting colors per toy. Keep plastic toys in primary palette, wooden toys in warm wood tones.

### Food
**Primary approach**: Bright, appetizing, natural colors.
**Avoid**: Gray, brown (except chocolate/bread), or dull tones.
**Best practices**: Fruits use their natural colors saturated 20% higher than real life. Prepared foods use warm golden tones.

### Furniture
**Primary approach**: Pastel and wood tones with occasional bright accents.
**Avoid**: Dark adult colors (navy, maroon, charcoal) as primary.
**Best practices**: Children's furniture is pastel colored. Wood furniture uses Warm Oak or Light Maple. Bright colors for accent pieces.

### School Supplies
**Primary approach**: Bright learning-friendly colors, mix of primary and pastel.
**Avoid**: Boring office colors (beige, gray, olive).
**Best practices**: Use primary colors for tools (pencils, scissors), pastel for furniture (desks, shelves).

### Nature
**Primary approach**: Bright organic greens, blues, earth tones, floral colors.
**Avoid**: Drab, muddy, or desaturated colors.
**Best practices**: Green should be vibrant (not olive or khaki). Flowers use the full rainbow. Sky is bright blue.

### Animals
**Primary approach**: Recognizable natural colors with 20-30% more saturation.
**Avoid**: Dull, realistic, or mutated colors.
**Best practices**: Use the animal's natural palette but brighter. Farm animals in pastel tones or classic colors. Exaggerate cuteness with pink cheeks and light bellies.

### Holidays
**Primary approach**: Traditional holiday colors at maximum saturation.
**Avoid**: Faded, dusty, or muted holiday colors.
**Best practices**: Christmas = red + green + gold. Halloween = orange + purple + black (friendly). Easter = full pastel palette. Birthday = rainbow.

## Color Psychology for Children's Content

| Color | Psychological Effect |
|---|---|
| Red | Excitement, energy, urgency — use sparingly |
| Blue | Calm, trust, stability — good for backgrounds |
| Yellow | Happiness, warmth, optimism — excellent for focus items |
| Green | Growth, health, nature — calming and balancing |
| Orange | Enthusiasm, creativity, adventure |
| Purple | Magic, imagination, wonder |
| Pink | Nurturing, sweetness, gentleness |
| White | Cleanliness, simplicity, clarity |
| Pastels | Safety, comfort, gentleness |

## Color Combination Rules

1. **High contrast**: Pair primary colors with white or pastel backgrounds for readability
2. **Warm + Cool balance**: Avoid all-warm or all-cool palettes in a single asset
3. **3-color max**: Limit each asset to 3 colors maximum (primary, secondary, accent)
4. **Wood tones count as neutral**: Wood-colored assets can pair with any palette
5. **Pastel + bright**: Pastel bases with bright accents create the signature Cocomelon-style look
6. **Avoid pure black**: Use `#333333` or `#1A1A1A` for shadows instead of `#000000`
