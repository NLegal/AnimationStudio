# Perspective Guide

## Design Philosophy

Camera perspective choices have a powerful emotional effect on the viewer. In Little Learning Town, perspectives should always keep the world feeling **approachable, safe, and child-friendly**. Avoid dramatic angles that disorient or intimidate young viewers.

---

## Eye-Level

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Description** | The camera is at the character's eye height. The most neutral and natural perspective. The viewer sees the world as another character would. Feels equal, connected, and grounded. |
| **When to Use** | Dialogue scenes, everyday activities, teaching moments, friendly interactions, standard storytelling |
| **Emotional Effect** | Neutral, comfortable, equal, connected, natural, grounded, approachable |

```
    ─── ─── ─── Camera at character eye height
         ●
        /
      (Character)
```

---

## Low Angle

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Description** | The camera looks up at the subject from below. Makes characters or objects appear larger, more important, or more impressive. Use gently — never to intimidate in this world. |
| **When to Use** | Introducing a tall building, showing a grown-up from a child's view, making something look impressive, heroic moments |
| **Emotional Effect** | Makes subject look big, important, impressive, admirable. Gives a child's perspective looking up at adults. |

```
         ●
        /|\
        / \      ← Subject looks large
       ───
      Camera
    (looking up)
```

---

## High Angle

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Description** | The camera looks down at the subject from above. Makes subjects appear smaller, softer, or more vulnerable. In a gentle way, this creates sympathy or shows a broader overview. |
| **When to Use** | Showing a character in a large space, overview of a scene, child sitting on the floor, bedtime scenes, playing with toys |
| **Emotional Effect** | Subject looks small, sweet, vulnerable (in a gentle way), creates overview understanding, soft sympathy |

```
      Camera
    (looking down)
       ───
         ●
        / \
      Subject appears smaller
```

---

## Bird's Eye (Top-Down)

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Description** | The camera looks straight down at the scene. A direct overhead view. Perfect for showing layouts, maps, table activities, cooking, and craft projects. Very clear and informational. |
| **When to Use** | Map exploration, table games, cooking/baking prep, drawing/coloring on desk, arranging toys, showing a town layout |
| **Emotional Effect** | Informational, clear, overview, playful (like looking at a dollhouse), objective, organized |

```
      Camera
        ↓
    ┌────────┐
    │  ●     │     ← Top-down view of character/objects
    │    ●   │
    │  ●     │
    └────────┘
```

---

## Worm's Eye

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Description** | The camera is on the ground looking straight up. A very dramatic, unusual perspective. Makes everything look enormous. Use sparingly for special dramatic or wonder-filled moments. |
| **When to Use** | Looking up at a tall tree, a giant building, a hot air balloon, fireworks, a tall character from lying on ground |
| **Emotional Effect** | Dramatic, awe-inspiring, wonder-filled, makes world feel big and impressive, creates childlike wonder |

```
        ☁️
       /  \
      /    \       ← Very tall subject
     /      \
    /        \
   ───────────
      Camera
    (looking up from ground)
```
