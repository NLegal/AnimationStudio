# Dialogue Engine Guide

## Purpose

The Dialogue Engine produces all spoken content in an episode: narration, character dialogue, questions directed at the audience, repetition for reinforcement, and vocabulary introduction. Every line of dialogue follows strict rules to ensure it is appropriate, educational, and engaging for young children.

---

## Dialogue Rules

All dialogue in every episode must follow these rules:

| Rule | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Short sentences** | Maximum 8-10 words per sentence for ages 2-4; 10-15 for ages 5-6 |
| **Simple grammar** | Subject-verb-object structure. No complex clauses. |
| **Positive tone** | Encouraging, warm, supportive. Never negative or critical. |
| **No sarcasm** | Children under 7 cannot understand sarcasm. Be literal. |
| **No slang** | Use standard language only. No regional slang. |
| **No abstract concepts** | Use concrete examples children can see and touch. |
| **Active voice** | "Lily throws the ball" not "The ball is thrown by Lily." |
| **Present tense** | Prefer present tense for immediacy. |
| **Clear pronoun references** | Avoid ambiguous "he" or "it." Name the character or object. |
| **Repetition of key terms** | Repeat the learning objective word 4-6 times naturally. |

---

## Sentence Length by Character Age

| Character Age | Max Sentence Length | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 2 years | 3-5 words | "Blue ball." "My turn." |
| 3 years | 5-7 words | "I see a blue ball." "Can I have a turn?" |
| 4 years | 7-10 words | "Look at the big blue ball. It is so pretty!" |
| 5 years | 10-12 words | "I found a blue ball behind the flowers. Is it yours?" |
| 6 years | 12-15 words | "The blue ball rolled all the way down the hill. I can go get it for you." |
| Adult (parent/teacher) | 10-12 words | "Great counting, Lily! Can you count the blue flowers too?" |

## Character Voice Patterns

### Lily Bunny (Age 4)

- Enthusiastic, curious, eager
- Often asks questions
- Uses expressive phrases: "Ooooh!", "Look!", "I can do it!"
- Asks for help when stuck
- Speaks in 5-8 word sentences

**Voice examples:**
- "Look at the pretty flowers!"
- "I want to count them — one, two, three!"
- "Can you help me, please?"
- "I did it! Yay!"

### Ben Bear (Age 4)

- Gentle, thoughtful, observant
- Speaks slower, uses thinking pauses
- Encourages others: "You can do it!"
- States facts: "This is a triangle."
- Speaks in 5-8 word sentences

**Voice examples:**
- "I see a big triangle."
- "Let me think about it..."
- "Good job, Lily!"
- "The block is blue, like the sky."

### Daisy Duck (Age 3)

- Cheerful, playful, energetic
- Repeats words for emphasis
- Uses shorter sentences
- Loves sounds and music
- Speaks in 3-6 word sentences

**Voice examples:**
- "Quack! Quack!"
- "Pretty flower!"
- "My turn? My turn?"
- "Dance with me!"

### Miss Owl (Adult / Teacher)

- Warm, patient, wise
- Asks guiding questions: "What shape is this?"
- Explains concepts simply
- Praises effort: "Great trying!"
- Speaks in 8-12 word sentences

**Voice examples:**
- "This is a triangle. It has three sides."
- "Let's count together. One, two, three."
- "What do you think happens next?"
- "Wonderful sharing, Lily!"

### Mama Bear (Adult / Parent)

- Nurturing, supportive, calm
- Gives gentle instructions
- Comforts when needed
- Uses repetition for routine
- Speaks in 8-10 word sentences

**Voice examples:**
- "Time to brush your teeth, Ben."
- "First we wash, then we eat."
- "You tried so hard. I am proud of you."
- "Good night, sleep tight."

### Grandpa (Adult / Elder)

- Warm, storytelling tone
- Shares wisdom through simple stories
- Uses gentle humor: "My sneeze sounds like a train!"
- Patient and unhurried
- Speaks in 8-12 word sentences

**Voice examples:**
- "When I was little, I loved the rain."
- "Let me tell you about the stars..."
- "Achoo! That was a big one!"

## Narration Style

- Third person, present tense
- Describes actions clearly: "Lily Bunny hops to the garden."
- Uses engaging tone, not flat
- Includes sound effects: "Pop! The balloon flies up!"
- Bridges scenes smoothly
- Never breaks the fourth wall (interactions are for characters, not narrator)

## Dialogue Structure for Scenes

Every episode follows a standard scene progression:

### 1. Introduction Scene

**Purpose:** Greet the audience, establish setting, introduce characters.

**Dialogue pattern:**
- Narration sets the scene
- Characters greet each other
- Character states goal or feeling
- Invitation to the audience

**Example:**
```
Narrator: "It is a sunny morning in Sunny Meadows."
Lily: "Good morning, everyone!"
Ben: "What a beautiful day!"
Lily: "Today I want to count things."
Lily: "Can you count with me?"
```

### 2. Teaching Scene

**Purpose:** Present the learning objective.

**Dialogue pattern:**
- Identify the concept
- Show multiple examples
- Label and describe
- Ask the audience to participate

**Example:**
```
Miss Owl: "This is the color blue."
Miss Owl: "The sky is blue. The ocean is blue."
Miss Owl: "Can you point to something blue?"
Ben: "The flower is blue!"
Miss Owl: "Yes! The flower is blue."
```

### 3. Practice Scene

**Purpose:** Reinforce through repetition and interaction.

**Dialogue pattern:**
- Characters practice together
- Audience prompted to join
- Mistakes corrected gently
- Success celebrated

**Example:**
```
Lily: "Let's count the apples."
Lily: "One... two... three!"
Ben: "I see four apples!"
Lily: "Yes! One, two, three, four!"
Lily: "Can you count with us?"
```

### 4. Celebration Scene

**Purpose:** Positive reinforcement, song or dance.

**Dialogue pattern:**
- Express joy and pride
- Recap what was learned
- Sing or dance together
- Thank the audience

**Example:**
```
Ben: "We did it!"
Lily: "We learned to count to four!"
All: Hooray!
Narrator: "And that is how Lily and Ben learned to count."
```

### 5. Goodbye Scene

**Purpose:** Wrap up, reinforce lesson, farewell.

**Dialogue pattern:**
- Final recap of learning
- Characters say goodbye
- Encouragement to practice
- Preview hint (optional)

**Example:**
```
Lily: "Counting is so fun!"
Ben: "Count everything you see today!"
Lily: "Goodbye, friends! See you next time!"
Narrator: "Keep counting, little learners!"
```

## Educational Reinforcement in Dialogue

To reinforce the learning objective, key vocabulary must appear naturally in dialogue:

**For "Learn the Color Blue":**
- "Look at the blue sky." (opening)
- "The blue flower is pretty." (exploration)
- "Is this blue? Yes! Blue balloon." (practice)
- "We see so many blue things!" (celebration)
- "Can you find something blue at home?" (goodbye)

The target word ("blue") should appear **4-6 times** organically, never in a forced context.

## Interactive Dialogue Patterns

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Can you...? | "Can you clap your hands?" |
| What is...? | "What color is this?" |
| Let's... | "Let's count together!" |
| Find the... | "Can you find the triangle?" |
| Say it with me | "Say 'blue' with me!" |
| Show me... | "Show me three fingers!" |
| Do you see...? | "Do you see the yellow star?" |
| What comes next? | "What comes after three?" |

These patterns appear at planned intervals with built-in pause timing for audience response.
