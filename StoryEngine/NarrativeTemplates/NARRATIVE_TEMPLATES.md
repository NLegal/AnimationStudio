# Narrative Templates Guide

## Purpose

Narrative Templates convert the abstract story grammar into a concrete, scene-by-scene episode structure. While the grammar defines the story's underlying logic, the narrative template defines its dramatic flow — the sequence of scenes, emotional beats, and audience experiences.

---

## The Standard Story Framework

This framework applies to every episode. It is the universal structure that makes every episode feel cohesive and complete.

```
Opening
  ↓
Goal
  ↓
Problem
  ↓
Discovery
  ↓
Learning
  ↓
Practice
  ↓
Success
  ↓
Celebration
  ↓
Goodbye
```

### Scene Breakdown

| Scene | Purpose | Duration | Emotional State |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | Greet the audience, set the scene, establish mood | 30-45s | Curiosity, excitement |
| Goal | Show what the character wants or needs | 20-30s | Anticipation |
| Problem | Introduce the challenge or conflict | 20-30s | Concern, thinking |
| Discovery | Find the solution or get help | 20-30s | Hope, realization |
| Learning | The educational core — teach the concept | 30-60s | Focus, engagement |
| Practice | Audience and character practice together | 30-45s | Participation, confidence |
| Success | Character succeeds using what they learned | 15-25s | Pride, joy |
| Celebration | Happy resolution, song or dance | 30-45s | Joy, satisfaction |
| Goodbye | Wrap up, reinforce lesson, farewell | 15-20s | Warmth, contentment |

## Template Structures by Episode Type

### Educational Song Episode

**Focus:** Teaching through music and repetition

```
[Opening] Upbeat musical introduction. Characters wave hello.
[Goal] "Today we're going to learn about..."
[Verse 1] Introduce concept with simple lyrics
[Chorus] Catchy repeated refrain with actions
[Verse 2] More examples of the concept
[Chorus] Repeat refrain
[Bridge] Apply the concept in a fun way
[Chorus] Final big chorus
[Goodbye] Gentle outro, lesson recap
```

**Duration:** 2-3 minutes
**Learning Reinforcement:** Concept repeated 5-7 times in song
**Best For:** Alphabet, numbers, colors, shapes, animal sounds

**Example:** "The Counting Song" — count to five with finger actions, each verse adds one more number.

---

### Story Time Episode

**Focus:** Narrative with embedded educational content

```
[Opening] "Once upon a time in Sunny Meadows..."
[Character Intro] Meet the characters and their situation
[Problem] Something goes wrong or needs solving
[Teaching Moment] A friend or teacher explains the concept
[Practice] Characters try using what they learned
[Solution] The problem is solved through learning
[Celebration] "Yay! We did it!"
[Lesson Recap] "Today we learned..."
[Goodbye] Warm farewell, see you next time
```

**Duration:** 3-5 minutes
**Learning Reinforcement:** Concept appears in dialogue 3-4 times
**Best For:** Friendship, emotions, problem solving, healthy habits

**Example:** "Lily Bunny Learns to Share" — Lily has one toy, Ben wants a turn. Miss Owl helps them learn to share.

---

### Morning Routine Episode

**Focus:** Daily activities with step-by-step structure

```
[Opening] Wake up, stretch, greet the day
[Step 1] Get dressed — what to wear
[Step 2] Eat breakfast — healthy food choices
[Step 3] Brush teeth — proper brushing technique
[Step 4] Pack bag — what to bring
[Step 5] Go to school / playground — transition
[Song] Morning routine song with actions
[Goodbye] Ready for the day!
```

**Duration:** 3-4 minutes
**Learning Reinforcement:** Sequential steps repeated twice
**Best For:** Healthy habits, daily routines, independence

**Example:** "Ben Bear's Morning" — Ben learns to get ready by himself with encouragement from Mama Bear.

---

### Adventure Episode

**Focus:** Exploration, imagination, discovery

```
[Opening] Characters gather with excitement
[Imagination Trigger] "Let's pretend..."
[Departure] Set off on the adventure
[Discovery 1] First interesting encounter
[Challenge] An imaginary obstacle
[Teamwork] Work together to overcome
[Discovery 2] More learning along the way
[Return] Back home safely
[Reflection] "What did we learn?"
[Goodbye] Until the next adventure
```

**Duration:** 4-6 minutes
**Learning Reinforcement:** Concept appears in adventure context 3-4 times
**Best For:** Science, space, ocean, geography, animals, imagination

**Example:** "Space Adventure with Lily" — Lily and Ben build a rocket and visit planets, counting stars along the way.

---

### Problem-Solving Episode

**Focus:** Working through a challenge step by step

```
[Opening] Characters are playing happily
[Discovery] Something is wrong / missing / broken
[Feeling] Express emotion about the problem
[Think] "What can we do?"
[Plan] Come up with ideas
[Try] Attempt a solution
[Adjust] If it doesn't work, try differently
[Success] The solution works!
[Lesson] "When we have a problem, we can..."
[Goodbye] Proud and happy
```

**Duration:** 3-5 minutes
**Learning Reinforcement:** Problem-solving steps repeated
**Best For:** Problem solving, emotions, persistence

**Example:** "Daisy Duck's Lost Teddy" — Daisy can't find Teddy before bed. The friends search together using a step-by-step plan.

---

### Song-and-Dance Episode

**Focus:** Music, movement, and physical activity

```
[Opening] Upbeat instrumental, energetic greeting
[Warm-up] Simple stretches or clapping
[Song 1] Catchy song with dance moves
[Teach Moves] Break down the dance step by step
[Practice] Do the moves together slowly
[Song 2] Another song, faster tempo
[Full Dance] Combine all moves
[Freeze Game] Fun pause activity
[Cool Down] Gentle movements, breathing
[Goodbye] Wave and sing farewell
```

**Duration:** 3-5 minutes
**Learning Reinforcement:** Physical repetition of motor skills
**Best For:** Motor skills, dance, music, coordination

## Customizing Templates

### Variable Slots

Every template has customizable slots:

```
[Character] — Main character (Lily Bunny, Ben Bear, Daisy Duck)
[Location] — Where the episode takes place
[Learning Objective] — The specific concept taught
[Theme] — The episode theme
[Conflict] — The specific problem
[Asset List] — Required props
[Songs] — Song type and placement
[Interactive Moments] — Audience participation prompts
```

### Customization Rules

1. **One variable changed per slot** — Keep the template structure intact
2. **Character voice consistency** — Dialogue must match each character's established voice
3. **Learning objective determines teaching approach** — Counting uses repetition; emotions use discussion
4. **Theme modifies visual and asset choices** — Same template, different visual flavor
5. **Age target modifies vocabulary complexity** — Simpler words for younger audiences

## Title Generation Patterns

| Pattern | Formula | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Character Learns | `[Character] [Action] [Concept]` | Lily Bunny Learns to Share |
| Character's Adventure | `[Character]'s [Adventure Type]` | Ben Bear's Space Adventure |
| The [Concept] [Activity] | `The [Adjective] [Noun]` | The Colorful Kite, The Lost Teddy |
| Let's [Action] | `Let's [Verb] [Something]` | Let's Count Together, Let's Plant a Garden |
| [Character] and the [Item] | `[Character] and the [Item]` | Daisy Duck and the Missing Cookie |
| Today We [Learn/Do] | `Today We [Action] [Concept]` | Today We Learn About Shapes |
| [Action] with [Character] | `[Verb] with [Character]` | Sing with Lily, Count with Ben |
| [Character]'s [Event] | `[Character]'s [Special Event]` | Daisy Duck's Birthday, Lily's Picnic |

### Title Rules

- **Keep it simple** — 3-6 words maximum
- **Include the main character** when possible (builds brand recognition)
- **Avoid negatives** in titles (no "Don't" or "Can't")
- **Make it clear** what the episode is about
- **Use the learning objective** when it fits naturally
