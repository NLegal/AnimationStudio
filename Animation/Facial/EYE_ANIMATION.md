# Eye Animation Standards

## Eyes Are the Focal Point

In preschool animation, eyes are the single most important feature. Children instinctively follow eye gaze to understand attention, intention, and emotion. Well-executed eye animation makes characters feel alive and trustworthy.

Every character's eyes must be animated with intentionality — never random, never static.

---

## Blink Frequency

| Metric            | Value                |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| Blink interval    | Every 4–6 seconds    |
| Blinks per minute | 6–15                 |
| Hold closed       | 2 frames minimum     |

- Characters in thought or listening: lean toward every 5–6 seconds
- Characters excited or surprised: blinks may become more frequent (every 3–4 seconds)
- Characters sad or sleepy: blinks slower and heavier (every 6–8 seconds)

---

## Blink Duration

| Blink Type     | Frames (at 24fps) | Duration | Usage                          |
|----------------|-------------------|----------|--------------------------------|
| Normal blink   | 2–3               | ~100ms   | Default                        |
| Slow blink     | 4–6               | ~200ms   | Tired, relaxed, content        |
| Double blink   | 2+2 (+4 gap)      | ~300ms   | Emphasis, surprise, processing |
| Fast blink     | 1–2               | ~60ms    | Startle, quick reaction        |
| Exaggerated    | 6–8               | ~300ms   | Comedic, Owl signature         |

- Blink timing: close takes 1 frame, hold closed 1–2 frames, open takes 1 frame
- Offset blinks between characters: never blink two characters in the same frame unless intentional

---

## Double Blink

Use for:
- Emphasis on a word or realization ("Wait — I have an idea!")
- Surprise that resolves into happiness
- Processing unexpected information
- Comedic reaction

Pattern: blink → pause 4–6 frames → blink again

---

## Eye Tracking Rules

Eyes must lead action, not follow it.

### Looking at Speaker During Conversation
- 90% of time: eyes directed at the speaking character
- Remainder: brief glances away for thinking, emphasis, or reaction
- When multiple characters speak, shift gaze to the current speaker within 4–6 frames of them starting

### Anticipatory Gaze (Look Before Reach)
- Eyes shift to object 6–8 frames before hand begins reaching
- This tells the child "the character wants that thing" before the action happens
- Critical for object interaction clarity

### Look in Direction of Movement
- Before walking, eyes shift in movement direction 4 frames ahead
- During turns, eyes lead the head by 2–4 frames
- When looking up/down, eyes move first, head follows

### Avoid Staring at Camera
- Direct address to camera is reserved for specific moments (song intros, direct questions)
- Default: characters look at each other or at objects in the scene
- When speaking to audience, hold for 12–24 frames, then glance away

---

## Eye Darts (Saccades)

| Property       | Value                |
|----------------|----------------------|
| Duration       | 2 frames             |
| Distance       | 10–30° visual angle  |
| Hold after     | 6–12 frames minimum  |

Use for:
- Thinking or searching for an answer
- Processing a question
- Slight nervousness or uncertainty
- Following a moving object

Pattern: quick shift → hold → quick shift (2–3 darts then settle)

---

## Eye Contact Rules

| Situation              | Eye Contact % | Break Every |
|------------------------|---------------|-------------|
| Listening              | 60%           | 5–7 seconds |
| Speaking (casual)      | 50%           | 4–6 seconds |
| Speaking (earnest)     | 70%           | 6–8 seconds |
| Nervous/embarrassed    | 30%           | 2–4 seconds |
| Confident/happy        | 60%           | 5–7 seconds |

### Gaze Direction Cues
- Look up and to the right: thinking, recalling
- Look down: shy, embarrassed, sad, processing
- Look to the side (left/right): distracted, listening, considering
- Direct center: earnest, confident, addressing

---

## Wide Eyes

| Phase      | Frames | Description               |
|------------|--------|---------------------------|
| Open       | 4–6    | Eyelids rise to level 4–5 |
| Hold       | 12–24  | Held for audience to register |
| Release    | 4–6    | Return to normal           |

Wide eyes are a powerful storytelling tool. Holding for at least 12 frames ensures preschool viewers recognize the emotion. Longer holds (18–24 frames) for major reveals or surprises.

---

## Squinting

| Phase      | Frames | Description                    |
|------------|--------|--------------------------------|
| Achieve    | 6      | Lower lids rise, brows lower   |
| Hold       | As needed | Varies by context            |
| Release    | 4      | Return to normal                |

Use for:
- Suspicion or skepticism
- Examining something closely
- Strong happiness (with smile)
- Bright light or frustration

---

## Tears

| Phase          | Frames | Description                        |
|----------------|--------|------------------------------------|
| Eyes welling   | 4–6    | Eyes become glossy/shiny            |
| Tear forms     | 6      | Tear appears at lower eyelid        |
| Tear rolls     | 12–18  | Tear travels down cheek             |
| Second tear    | 6–8    | Additional tears follow             |
| Stop crying    | 8–12   | Eyes clear, drying                  |

- Tears should be stylized: round, glossy, visible against fur/skin
- Eye level 1–2 during crying (closed or half-lidded squeezed)
- Blinking is more frequent and heavier during crying

## Per-Character Eye Notes

### Bunny
- Largest eyes in the cast — every eye movement reads bigger
- Extra white visible means eye level 4–5 is extremely expressive
- Ears follow gaze direction (ears perk up when looking up, relax when looking down)
- Blink can be slightly slower (3–4 frames) to emphasize softness

### Pig
- Smaller eyes relative to face — expressions need to be clear to read
- Eyes can squint nearly shut for happy (with snout wrinkles)
- Blink duration normal (2–3 frames)
- Nostrils visible in profile when looking to the side

### Owl
- Huge, wide-set eyes — exaggerated blinking is a character signature
- Can hold blink closed for 6–8 frames for comedic effect
- Eyes cannot track to extreme edges (biologically inspired limitation)
- Head turns to compensate for limited eye range
- Third eyelid (nictitating membrane) visible as a horizontal blink variant

### Bear
- Deep-set eyes with heavy brows — shadows form under brows in anger/thinking
- Warm brown irises reflect light well for emotional brightness
- Blink subtle due to fur texture (but still animate)
- Eye crinkles critical for genuine warmth

### Cat
- Slit pupils change with emotion and light
  - Wide/dilated: surprise, excitement, dark
  - Narrow slits: content, annoyed, bright light
- Eyes glow slightly in low light (stylized, not realistic)
- Slow blink = trust and affection (hold 6–8 frames)
- Stare intensity: cats can hold gaze longer than other characters (6–10 seconds)

### Duck
- Eyes are on sides of head — both eyes rarely visible at once
- When one eye is visible, it appears large on the head
- Blink is quick (2 frames) — no eyelashes visible
- Eye rings (white outline) accentuate expression
- Head tilt is more important than eye movement for expression
