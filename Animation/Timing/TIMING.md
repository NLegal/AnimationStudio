# Timing & Pacing Standards

## Version 1.0 — AI Nursery Studio

---

## Introduction

Preschool audiences need **more time** than older viewers to process what they see on screen. Every timing decision at AI Nursery Studio prioritizes comprehension over pacing.

**Core Principles:**
- Slower is safer — when in doubt, hold longer
- Every shot must be long enough for a child to observe and understand
- Pacing varies by content type (story, educational, song)
- Rhythm should feel calm and unhurried

**Frame Rate:** All values assume 24 fps unless otherwise specified.

---

## Preschool Pacing Foundation

| Age Group | Relative Pacing | Notes |
|-----------|----------------|-------|
| 2-4 years old | 100% baseline (slowest) | Needs 50% more hold time than 5-6 year olds |
| 4-5 years old | 75% of 2-4 baseline | Can process slightly faster cuts |
| 5-6 years old | 66% of 2-4 baseline | Can follow faster pacing but still slower than adult |

**Rule:** If targeting mixed ages (2-6), use the 2-4 year old baseline.

**Pacing Multiplier Reference:**
```
Target Age:  2yr  3yr  4yr  5yr  6yr  Adult
Multiplier:  1.5x 1.3x 1.0x 0.8x 0.7x 0.4x
```

## Shot Hold Times

| Shot Type | Minimum Hold | Recommended Hold | Maximum Hold |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Establishing Shot | 3 seconds (72 frames) | 4 seconds (96 frames) | 6 seconds (144 frames) |
| Wide Shot | 2 seconds (48 frames) | 3 seconds (72 frames) | 5 seconds (120 frames) |
| Medium Shot | 2 seconds (48 frames) | 3-4 seconds (72-96 frames) | 6 seconds (144 frames) |
| Close-Up | 1.5 seconds (36 frames) | 2-3 seconds (48-72 frames) | 4 seconds (96 frames) |
| Over-the-Shoulder | 2 seconds (48 frames) | 3 seconds (72 frames) | 5 seconds (120 frames) |
| Tracking Shot | 3 seconds (72 frames) | 4-5 seconds (96-120 frames) | 8 seconds (192 frames) |
| Two-Shot | 2 seconds (48 frames) | 3-4 seconds (72-96 frames) | 6 seconds (144 frames) |
| Group Shot | 3 seconds (72 frames) | 4 seconds (96 frames) | 6 seconds (144 frames) |
| Insert Shot | 1.5 seconds (36 frames) | 2 seconds (48 frames) | 3 seconds (72 frames) |

**Rule of Thumb:** When editing, hold each shot 30-50% longer than you would for adult content. If a cut feels too slow, it is probably still too fast for a preschooler.

## Reaction Time

Children need time to see an event AND process the character's response.

| Event Type | Character Reaction Delay | Total Audience Processing Time |
|------------|------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Surprise event | 8-12 frames | 1-2 seconds |
| Funny moment | 10-14 frames | 2-3 seconds |
| Sad moment | 12-16 frames | 2-4 seconds |
| Question asked | 8-12 frames pause before answer | 2-3 seconds |
| Object appears | 6-10 frames to notice | 1-2 seconds |
| Character enters | 8-12 frames to acknowledge | 1-2 seconds |

**Reaction Sequence:**
```
Event happens
  → [4-8 frames: Character processes]
  → [Character expression changes]
  → [4-8 frames: Character acts on reaction]
```

**Example — Surprise:**
```
Frame   0: Balloon pops
Frame   8: Character's eyes widen (startle begins)
Frame  12: Character jumps back
Frame  20: Character's expression shifts to surprise
Frame  28: Character looks at balloon remnants (curiosity)
Frame  40: Character says "Oh!"
```

## Action Timing

Actions need enough frames to read clearly.

| Action Type | Minimum Frames | Recommended Frames | Readability |
|-------------|---------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Simple reach | 6 frames | 10-12 frames | High |
| Pick up object | 8 frames | 12-16 frames | High |
| Walk across room | Variable | 24-48 frames | High |
| Jump | 8 frames | 12-16 frames | High |
| Fall down | 12 frames | 16-20 frames | High |
| Sit down | 8 frames | 12-16 frames | High |
| Stand up | 8 frames | 12-16 frames | High |
| Turn head | 4 frames | 6-8 frames | High |
| Wave | 8 frames | 12-16 frames | High |
| Point | 6 frames | 8-12 frames | High |
| Hug | 12 frames | 16-24 frames | High |
| Complex interaction | 16 frames | 20-30 frames | Medium-High |

**Action Readability Check:**
1. Can you see the full arc of motion?
2. Can you identify the start, middle, and end?
3. Does the anticipation read before the action?
4. Is the follow-through visible after the action?

If any answer is "no," add 4-6 frames.

## Emotional Beats

Give the audience time to feel.

| Emotion | Onset Time | Hold Time | Recovery |
|---------|-----------|-----------|----------|
| Happiness | 4-6 frames | 1-3 seconds | 8-12 frames |
| Surprise | 2-4 frames | 1-2 seconds | 4-8 frames |
| Sadness | 8-12 frames | 2-4 seconds | 12-16 frames |
| Excitement | 4-6 frames | 2-3 seconds | 8-12 frames |
| Curiosity | 6-8 frames | 2-3 seconds | 8-12 frames |
| Frustration | 8-12 frames | 1-2 seconds | 12-16 frames |
| Fear (mild) | 4-8 frames | 1-2 seconds | 8-12 frames |
| Contentment | 6-8 frames | 2-4 seconds | 8-12 frames |

**Emotional Beat Structure:**
```
[Setup] → [Trigger] → [Onset] → [Hold] → [Recovery] → [Next Beat]
  2-3s      1-2s       4-12f     1-4s      8-16f
```

**Example — Sad Moment:**
```
Setup:      Character sees broken toy (2 seconds)
Trigger:    Toy piece falls off (12 frames)
Onset:      Eyes well up, lip trembles (8 frames)
Hold:       Quiet sadness, looking at toy (3 seconds)
Recovery:   Friend arrives, comfort begins (12 frames)
```

## Pause Before Important Moments

Silence and stillness give weight to key story beats.

| Important Moment | Pause Duration | Purpose |
|-----------------|---------------|---------|
| Before a big reveal | 12-16 frames | Build anticipation |
| Before a character speaks | 6-10 frames | Prepare to listen |
| Before a question | 8-12 frames | Signal importance |
| After a question | 12-16 frames | Wait for audience to think |
| Before resolution | 12-16 frames | Build emotional payoff |
| Before a song starts | 8-12 frames | Transition to musical mode |

**Pause Structure:**
```
[Character stops moving]
  → [2-4 frames: Settle into stillness]
  → [8-12 frames: Complete stillness]
  → [Important moment begins]
```

**Rule:** A pause should feel uncomfortable to an adult editor — that means it is long enough for a child.

## Reading Time (Subtitles)

When text appears on screen (subtitles, labels, signs):

| Content Type | Reading Time | Calculation |
|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Single word | 1-2 seconds | 24-48 frames |
| Short phrase (2-3 words) | 2-3 seconds | 48-72 frames |
| Sentence (4-6 words) | 3-4 seconds | 72-96 frames |
| Label/name | 2-3 seconds | 48-72 frames |
| Subtitle line | 3-5 seconds | 72-120 frames |

**Reading Speed Formula:**
```
For preschool audiences:
  Hold time = (word count × 0.8 seconds) + 1.5 seconds base
  Minimum hold: 2 seconds

For song lyrics on screen:
  Hold time = matched to sung duration + 0.5 seconds
```

**Examples:**
```
"Ball" (1 word):  Hold 2.5 seconds (60 frames)
"Red ball" (2 words): Hold 3.5 seconds (84 frames)
"The ball is red" (4 words): Hold 4.5 seconds (108 frames)
```

**Rules:**
- Text must be readable in preschool-friendly font
- Text should appear after the spoken word (if voiceover)
- Hold text for 0.5 seconds after reading time for processing
- Animate text in gently (no abrupt appearance)

## Song Pacing

Musical segments follow different pacing rules.

| Element | Duration | Notes |
|---------|----------|-------|
| Intro (before singing) | 4-8 seconds | Musical setup, scene established |
| Per verse | 8-16 seconds | Varies by song |
| Per chorus | 8-12 seconds | Repeated sections |
| Bridge | 8-12 seconds | Change-up section |
| Outro | 4-8 seconds | Wind down, transition |

**Visual Beat Holds:**
| Song Moment | Hold Time | Purpose |
|-------------|-----------|---------|
| First note of chorus | 2-3 seconds | Establish the hook |
| Key lyric moment | 2-4 seconds | Emphasize meaning |
| Dance break | 4-8 seconds | Full visual showcase |
| Final note | 2-4 seconds | Satisfying resolution |
| Between verses | 1-2 seconds | Brief transition |

**Rules:**
- Visuals should change on musical phrases, not every beat
- Key visual beats (chorus, big moment): hold for full phrase
- Character mouth movements sync to lyrics
- Group shots during chorus, individual shots during verses
- On final note, hold last frame for 1-2 seconds

## Educational Content Pacing

Learning segments need maximum clarity.

| Concept Complexity | Duration | Explanation |
|-------------------|----------|-------------|
| Simple (color, shape, number) | 3-5 seconds | One concept at a time |
| Medium (matching, sorting) | 4-6 seconds | Show concept + example |
| Complex (cause/effect, sequence) | 5-8 seconds | Show step by step |

**Educational Segment Structure:**
```
1. Introduce concept (3-4 seconds)
2. Show example (4-5 seconds)
3. Label/name the concept (3-4 seconds)
4. Repeat example (3-4 seconds)
5. Check for understanding (4-6 seconds)
```

**Concept Repetition:**
```
First appearance:  Hold 4-5 seconds
Second appearance: Hold 3-4 seconds
Third appearance:  Hold 2-3 seconds
```

**Visual Learning Rules:**
- Education elements (letters, numbers, shapes) appear on screen for 1-2 seconds before being referenced
- Objects being taught should be shown alone first (isolated), then in context
- Counting: show each number for 1-2 seconds before moving to next
- Colors: show color swatch for 2 seconds before labeling

## Scene Pacing by Content Type

### Narrative Story
```
Establishing:  4 seconds (96 frames)
Scene setup:   3 seconds (72 frames)
Interaction:   4-6 seconds (96-144 frames)
Reaction:      2-3 seconds (48-72 frames)
Dialogue:      Per line, 3-5 seconds per exchange
Scene end:     2-3 seconds winding down
```

### Educational Segment
```
Intro concept:    4 seconds (96 frames)
Teach concept:    5-6 seconds (120-144 frames)
Practice:         4-5 seconds (96-120 frames)
Review:           3-4 seconds (72-96 frames)
Transition:       2 seconds (48 frames)
```

### Musical Number
```
Intro:          4-6 seconds (96-144 frames)
Verse 1:        12-16 seconds (288-384 frames)
Chorus 1:       10-12 seconds (240-288 frames)
Verse 2:        10-14 seconds (240-336 frames)
Chorus 2:       10-12 seconds (240-288 frames)
Bridge:         8-12 seconds (192-288 frames)
Final Chorus:   12-16 seconds (288-384 frames)
Outro:          4-6 seconds (96-144 frames)
```

### Activity/Game Segment
```
Setup:          3-4 seconds (72-96 frames)
Round 1:        6-8 seconds (144-192 frames)
Reaction:       2-3 seconds (48-72 frames)
Round 2:        5-7 seconds (120-168 frames)
Reaction:       2-3 seconds (48-72 frames)
Wrap-up:        3-4 seconds (72-96 frames)
```

## Quality Checklist

| Check | Pass/Fail |
|-------|-----------|
| Each shot is held long enough for comprehension | ☐ |
| Reactions include processing time (8-16 frames) | ☐ |
| Emotional beats have holds (1-4 seconds) | ☐ |
| Pauses precede important moments (12 frames) | ☐ |
| Subtitles have adequate reading time (3-5 seconds per line) | ☐ |
| Educational concepts are given 4-8 seconds | ☐ |
| Action timing allows readability (12-24 frames) | ☐ |
| Pacing matches target age group (2-4 = slower) | ☐ |
| Song visuals follow musical phrasing | ☐ |
| No cuts faster than 1.5 seconds | ☐ |
