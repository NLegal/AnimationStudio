# Pronunciation Dictionary

## Version 1.0

---

## Introduction

Consistent pronunciation builds brand identity. Children learn words by hearing them repeated the same way. When every episode, song, and voiceover uses the same pronunciation for character names, town names, and recurring objects, the studio develops a recognizable audio signature.

This dictionary serves as the authoritative reference for all voice actors, narrators, TTS systems, and localization teams. Each entry includes the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) representation, a simple pronunciation guide for non-linguists, and usage notes.

---

## Pronunciation Rules for Voice Actors

### Vowel Clarity
- All vowel sounds should be crisp but not exaggerated
- Maintain short vowels in words like "bunny" (/ˈbʌni/), not "bah-ny"
- Long vowels in "dream" (/drim/), "green" (/grin/), "play" (/pleɪ/)

### Consonant Precision
- Final consonants should be soft but present
- "Friends" ends with /dz/, not /z/
- "And" is fully pronounced (/ænd/), not reduced to "an"

### Rhythm and Pacing
- Character names get equal weight on first and last name
- Compound location names flow naturally with brief pauses between words
- Maintain age-appropriate tempo: not rushed, not dragging

### Emotional Color
- Happy moments: slightly higher pitch, bouncier rhythm
- Calm moments: lower pitch, smoother transitions
- Surprise: slightly wider pitch range, brief pause before key word
- Comfort: warm tone, gentle vowel lengthening on final syllables

## Localization Notes

When translating to other languages:

1. Preserve the syllable count of character names whenever possible
2. Maintain stress patterns for singability
3. Transliterate IPA into target language phonetics
4. Create separate pronunciation guide files for each locale
5. Re-record all entries in `/Audio/Localization/[lang]/PronunciationDictionary/`
