# Nursery Rhyme Writing Guide

## Overview

Nursery rhymes are the foundation of early childhood music. Their patterns — repetition, rhyme, simple meter, and call-and-response — are proven to aid language development, memory, and phonemic awareness. This guide shows how to write new nursery rhymes and adapt classic ones for Little Learning Town.

---

## Classic Rhyme Patterns

### Pattern A: Twinkle Twinkle Little Star (AABB + repeat)

```
Line 1 (A): Twinkle, twinkle, little star            [rhyme A]
Line 2 (A): How I wonder what you are                [rhyme A]
Line 3 (B): Up above the world so high                [rhyme B]
Line 4 (B): Like a diamond in the sky                 [rhyme B]
Line 5 (A): Twinkle, twinkle, little star             [rhyme A]
Line 6 (A): How I wonder what you are                 [rhyme A]
```

**Structure**: 6 lines, AABB with final couplet repeat
**Meter**: 7 syllables per line (trochaic: DUM-da DUM-da DUM-da DUM)
**Use**: Bedtime, wonder, gentle topics

### Pattern B: Row Row Row Your Boat (ABAB quatrain)

```
Line 1 (A): Row, row, row your boat                    [rhyme A - "boat"]
Line 2 (B): Gently down the stream                      [rhyme B - "stream"]
Line 3 (A): Merrily, merrily, merrily, merrily          [rhyme A - no exact rhyme, assonance]
Line 4 (B): Life is but a dream                         [rhyme B - "dream"]
```

**Structure**: 4 lines, ABAB
**Meter**: 2 stressed syllables per line, very short
**Use**: Action songs, repetitive activities

### Pattern C: Old MacDonald (Call and Response)

```
Line 1: Old MacDonald had a farm, E-I-E-I-O
Line 2: And on his farm he had a cow, E-I-E-I-O
Line 3: With a moo-moo here and a moo-moo there
Line 4: Here a moo, there a moo, everywhere a moo-moo
Line 5: Old MacDonald had a farm, E-I-E-I-O
```

**Structure**: 5 lines, verse structure with repeated refrain
**Meter**: Variable syllables, strong rhythm
**Use**: Animals, sounds, interactive learning

### Pattern D: Wheels on the Bus (Cumulative)

```
Line 1: The wheels on the bus go round and round
Line 2: Round and round, round and round
Line 3: The wheels on the bus go round and round
Line 4: All through the town
```

Repeated for each verse with different actions/parts (wipers, horn, people, etc.)
**Use**: Transportation, body parts, daily routines

### Pattern E: Baa Baa Black Sheep (Question-Answer)

```
Line 1 (Question): Baa, baa, black sheep, have you any wool?
Line 2 (Answer):   Yes sir, yes sir, three bags full
Line 3 (Detail):   One for the master, one for the dame
Line 4 (Detail):   And one for the little boy who lives down the lane
```

**Structure**: Question-answer, then elaboration
**Use**: Counting, sharing, conversational

## Rhyme Scheme Templates

### AABB (Couplets)

```
Apples in the morning, apples in the night    [A]
Eating apples makes me feel so bright         [A]
Apples are the fruit I love the best           [B]
Better than all the rest, they pass the test   [B]
```

**Feel**: Simple, predictable, easy to remember
**Best for**: Alphabet, colors, basic concepts

### ABAB (Alternating)

```
Sun is shining in the sky                      [A]
Flowers blooming on the ground                 [B]
Clouds are floating way up high                [A]
Birds are singing lovely sound                 [B]
```

**Feel**: More dynamic, feels like conversation
**Best for**: Nature, emotions, stories

### ABCB (Ballad)

```
Little bunny hopping by                         [A]
Stops to eat a carrot so sweet                  [B]
Wiggles nose and winks an eye                   [C]
Then hops along on little feet                  [B]
```

**Feel**: Story-like, less rigid
**Best for**: Narrative songs, character songs

### AABCCB (Limerick-like)

```
There once was a cat with a hat                  [A]
Who sat on a mat with a bat                      [A]
And he sang all day long                       [B]
His favorite song                             [C]
Which went ding-dong-ding-dong like that         [B]
```

**Feel**: Playful, silly
**Best for**: Fun songs, nonsense rhymes, character introductions

## Meter and Rhythm for Preschool Voices

### Understanding Meter

Meter is the pattern of stressed (DUM) and unstressed (da) syllables.

### Common Metrical Feet

| Foot | Pattern | Example | Use |
|------|---------|---------|-----|
| **Iamb** | da-DUM | "to-DAY" | Natural speech rhythm |
| **Trochee** | DUM-da | "TWI-kle" | Nursery rhyme feel |
| **Anapest** | da-da-DUM | "in the SKY" | Bouncy, fast |
| **Dactyl** | DUM-da-da | "MER-ri-ly" | Playful, rolling |

### Recommended Meter for Preschool

**Trochaic** (DUM-da) is the most natural for nursery rhymes. It matches clapping patterns and is easiest for young children to repeat.

**Iambic** (da-DUM) is also good — it matches natural English speech.

**Line length**: 6–9 syllables per line is ideal for 2–4 year olds. Shorter is better.

### Counting Syllables

```
Twinkle twinkle little star          = 7 syllables ✓
How I wonder what you are            = 7 syllables ✓
Up above the world so high           = 7 syllables ✓
Like a diamond in the sky            = 7 syllables ✓
```

If a line is too long (10+ syllables), children will stumble. If too short (3–4 syllables), it sounds choppy.

## Adapting Existing Nursery Rhymes

### Method 1: Replace Subject, Keep Structure

Take "Twinkle Twinkle Little Star" and adapt for counting:

```
Original:  Twinkle, twinkle, little star
Adapted:   Counting, counting, numbers bright

Original:  How I wonder what you are
Adapted:   Let's count numbers day and night

Original:  Up above the world so high
Adapted:   One and two and three and four

Original:  Like a diamond in the sky
Adapted:   Let's count numbers, then some more
```

### Method 2: Keep Refrain, Change Verses

Keep "E-I-E-I-O" from Old MacDonald, change animal:

```
(Keep refrain)
Old MacDonald had a farm, E-I-E-I-O
And on his farm he had a [NEW ANIMAL], E-I-E-I-O
With a [SOUND]-[SOUND] here and a [SOUND]-[SOUND] there
Here a [SOUND], there a [SOUND], everywhere a [SOUND]-[SOUND]
Old MacDonald had a farm, E-I-E-I-O
```

### Method 3: Keep Melody Contour, Change Lyrics

Keep the rhythmic and melodic shape but write entirely new words. This works great for educational content because the tune is already familiar.

**Example: "Alphabet Thank You" (to tune of Twinkle Twinkle)**

```
Thank you, thank you, mommy dear
Thank you, thank you, daddy dear
For the love and care you give
Every single day I live
Thank you, thank you, mommy dear
Thank you, thank you, daddy dear
```

### Method 4: Mashup — Combine Two Rhymes

Combine structural elements from two classic rhymes:

```
(Verse structure from "Wheels on the Bus")
The numbers on the clock go tick-tock-tick
Tick-tock-tick, tick-tock-tick
The numbers on the clock go tick-tock-tick
All through the day

(Chorus structure from "Twinkle Twinkle")
One, two, three, and four
Let's count numbers, then some more
Five, six, seven, eight
Learning numbers is so great
```

## Quick Reference: Rhyme Writing Checklist

```
□ Lines are 6–9 syllables (shorter for younger children)
□ Strong downbeat on stressed syllables
□ Rhyme scheme is consistent throughout
□ Repetition of key phrases (3+ times)
□ One core concept per song
□ Call-and-response or repetition for participation
□ Positive, encouraging language
□ Age-appropriate vocabulary (no complex words)
□ Song ends with a clear, resolved feeling
□ Can be sung comfortably by a child (C4–C5 range)
```

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

| Pitfall | Why | Fix |
|---------|-----|-----|
| Uneven syllable count | Children can't keep the beat | Count syllables per line; match across verses |
| Forced rhymes | SOUND-like words that don't make sense | Use a rhyming dictionary or restructure the line |
| Passive voice | "The apple is eaten by me" | Use active: "I eat the apple" |
| Too many concepts | Song tries to teach everything | One concept per song, max two |
| Adult vocabulary | "Delicious," "magnificent," "extraordinary" | Use "yummy," "great," "super" |
| Long intros | Child loses interest | Intro ≤ 4 bars, ideally 2 bars |
| No clear ending | Song trails off | End with resolved chord, tag, or fade |
| Too many characters | Hard to follow | One main character per song |
| Abstract concepts | "Gravity" for a 2-year-old | Concrete: "What goes up comes down" |
