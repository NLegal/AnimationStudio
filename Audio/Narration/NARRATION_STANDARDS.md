# Narration Standards

> **Version:** 1.0
> **Series:** Little Learning Town
> **Studio:** AI Nursery Rhyme Studio

---

## Narrator Decision

**Single narrator** for Little Learning Town.

A single consistent voice builds the strongest brand recognition for preschool audiences.

---

## Narrator Character

### Voice Profile

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Character | The Little Learning Town Storyteller |
| Identity | A warm, friendly adult voice — timeless, not attributed to any specific character |
| Personality | Patient, encouraging, knowledgeable, warm |
| Pitch | Medium |
| Energy | Moderate |
| Speech Speed | Medium (with 2–3 second pauses between sentences) |
| Accent | Neutral |
| Voice Quality | Warm, clear, inviting, slightly resonant |
| TTS Model | Kokoro |
| Voice Notes | "Warm preschool narrator, calm pace, clear pronunciation, friendly tone, energetic but gentle, suitable for children ages 2–6." |
| Tone Philosophy | Warm, patient, never condescending — speaks to children with respect and joy |

### Why Single Narrator

- Children recognize the voice as a trusted guide
- Consistent audio branding across hundreds of episodes
- Simplifies production (one voice to record, mix, and master)
- Eliminates confusion for young viewers
- Matches the Cocomelon-inspired format

### Why Not Mommy Bunny or Teacher Owl

The narrator exists **outside** the story world. Mommy Bunny and Teacher Owl are characters within episodes. Using them as narrator would blur the line between story and guidance. The narrator is a **storyteller** who welcomes viewers, explains concepts, and wraps up lessons — then steps aside to let characters shine.

## When Narrator Speaks

### Episode Openings
- Standard: "Welcome to Little Learning Town!"
- Introduces the episode theme
- Sets the scene for what children will learn

### Scene Transitions
- Moving between locations: "Meanwhile, at the farm..."
- Time passing: "Later that afternoon..."
- Character introductions: "And here comes Daisy Duck!"
- Changes in activity: "Now it's time for a song!"

### Educational Explanations
- Did You Know? moments
- Concept introductions: "Sharing means everyone gets a turn."
- Fact delivery: "Did you know that apples float? They have air inside them!"
- Recap of learning: "Today we learned about the color blue."

### Moral / Lesson Summaries
- End-of-episode wrap-ups
- "And that's why sharing makes everyone happy!"
- "Remember, being kind starts with a smile."
- "What a wonderful day of learning!"

### Episode Closings
- "See you next time in Little Learning Town!"
- Gentle goodbye that signals the episode is ending

---

## When Narrator Stays Silent

The narrator is a guide, not a character. Silence is powerful in preschool content — it gives children space to process.

### During Character Dialogue
- Never interrupt or talk over characters
- Characters drive all emotional and social moments

### During Songs
- Songs are character moments
- Let the music breathe without narration
- Exception: narrator may introduce a song ("Let's all sing together!")

### During Emotional Moments
- Let silence or character reactions carry the emotion
- A character's hug, laugh, or tear needs no explanation
- Trust the visual and vocal performance

### During Call-and-Response
- When characters ask the audience a question
- Narrator does not answer — leaves space for the child

### During Repetition
- When a key word or phrase is repeated for learning
- Let the repetition land without additional commentary

## Narrator Tone Guidelines

| Principle | Guidance |
|-----------|----------|
| Warmth | Always sounds like a trusted friend |
| Patience | Never rushed — children need processing time |
| Respect | Never condescending or babyish |
| Clarity | Every word must be perfectly intelligible |
| Energy | Engaged but not overwhelming |
| Consistency | Same tone, pace, and energy across all episodes |
| Smile | Narrator should sound like they are smiling |
| Curiosity | Wonder and excitement about learning |

### What to Avoid

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Sarcasm | Confusing for preschool minds |
| Rushing | Causes anxiety in young viewers |
| Over-excitement | Overstimulating |
| Monotone | Loses engagement |
| Complex vocabulary | Creates confusion |
| Talking down | Disrespectful to children |
| Moralizing | Preachy tone turns children off |

## Pacing Guidelines

| Element | Duration | Notes |
|---------|----------|-------|
| Between sentences | 2–3 seconds | Allows processing |
| After a question | 3–4 seconds | Space for child to answer |
| Before a song | 2 seconds | Anticipation build |
| After a song | 2 seconds | Let the song settle |
| Scene transition | 3 seconds | Visual change + narration |
| Episode opening | 4–5 seconds | Title + welcome |
| Episode closing | 4–5 seconds | Goodbye + fade |
| Key word emphasis | Slight pause after | Reinforce learning |
| Emotional beat | Let silence hold | 2–3 seconds of quiet |

---

## Recording Levels

| Element | Target Level | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| Narrator dialogue | –6 dB to –3 dB | Consistent anchor point |
| Character dialogue | –6 dB to –3 dB | Matches narrator level |
| Songs (vocals) | –4 dB to –2 dB | Slightly louder for musical impact |
| Sound effects | –12 dB to –8 dB | Support, never overpower |
| Background ambience | –18 dB to –14 dB | Subtle texture |
| Music bed (under dialogue) | –18 dB to –12 dB | Clear speech priority |
| Mastering ceiling | –1 dB | Headroom for distribution |

### Mixing Priority

1. **Narrator / Character dialogue** — always clear and present
2. **Song vocals** — prominent over instruments
3. **Sound effects** — supportive, not distracting
4. **Background ambience** — felt, not heard
5. **Music beds** — atmosphere, never competition

## Narrator Script Format

Every narrator line in scripts should include:

```
[NARRATOR]
[2s pause]
"Welcome to Little Learning Town!"
[3s pause — scene establishes]
"Today, Lily and Ben are learning about sharing."
[2s pause]
```

Timing notes in brackets help editors and animators sync.

---

## Consistency Checklist

Every episode must verify:

- [ ] Narrator voice matches established profile
- [ ] Same warm, patient tone throughout
- [ ] 2–3 second pauses between sentences
- [ ] No interruption of character dialogue
- [ ] No narration during songs (except intro)
- [ ] Recording level at –6 dB to –3 dB
- [ ] Educational explanations are simple and clear
- [ ] Episode opening and closing follow templates
- [ ] Scene transitions use approved transition templates
- [ ] Moral summaries are positive and actionable
